2 · Business & Moat

Waaree manufactures solar PV modules — India's largest and, by its own claim, the world's largest non-Chinese module maker at ~25.8 GW — sold through utility/IPP/C&I (39.7% of Q1 FY27 revenue), retail/B2C (30.2%), overseas exports (21.2%) and EPC (8.9%). It earns a manufacturing margin on modules, increasingly on self-made cells (5.4 GW → 15.4 GW), and is expanding into BESS, inverters, transformers, T&D, solar glass and green hydrogen under "Waaree 2.0". The moat is scale plus DCR/ALMM qualification — real, but policy-rented and capital-intensive, and margins just compressed 800+ bps. AMBER

2A · Product & Portfolio

Mono PERC, TOPCon (G12/G12R), HJT (to 730 Wp), bifacial/flexible modules; cells; EPC/O&M; inverters (4.15 GW); transformers; BESS (5.15 GWh); electrolysers. Modules ≈93% of Q1 FY27 revenue; EPC ~8.9%. Realisation guided ~$0.24–0.25/W (long-range DCR ~$0.21–0.22/W). Module EBIT collapsed to 9.3% in Q1 FY27 from 14.0% QoQ. Top-5 customers ~27%, top-10 ~37%. Single product-specific risk: the module is a policy-priced commodity; durable margin lives in cells Waaree has barely begun to make at scale. AMBER

2B · Suppliers & Customers

Customer names not disclosed; ~35 GW cumulative deployed; ~40% India / 36% US-domestic / 24% India-exports. Wafer and cell ≈50% of raw-material cost, largely imported — the central input-price/FX exposure (mitigant: Oman polysilicon stake). Related-party route: Waaree Semicon acquired by a WOS from the promoter company (disclosed, AC-approved). Largest counterparty risk: the US channel, where the CBP matter sits. AMBER

3 · Industry & Cycle

India module capacity roughly doubled to ~74 GW in FY25 while policy steers demand to local supply (BCD, ALMM-I, ALMM-II for cells from Jun-26). The profit pool is shifting upstream to cells/wafers. FY26's 22.3% operating margin is a cyclical high vs a 4–5% trough; Q1 FY27's 18.15% is the first compression. Mid-cycle operating margin is more plausibly ~17–19%. AMBER

15 · Red-Team Short Report

Waaree is a commodity module-maker dressed as an integrated platform, and the cash is leaving the building. Earnings quality: CFO/PAT collapsed from 1.6x (FY25) to 0.42x (FY26); FCF went from −₹114 cr to −₹3,209 cr; inventory days 96→129; cash-conversion cycle 38→84. Profit is being manufactured on the accrual line while cash is absorbed by working capital. Margins are rolling over: Q1 FY27 operating margin −820 bps YoY to 18.15%, module EBIT halved to 9.3% from 14.0% QoQ. The US profit pool turned hostile: a June 2026 final evasion determination (EAPA 8163) citing a "four-year history of reporting the wrong country of origin" it called "material and false," with cash deposits up to 271.28% — and an AD/CVD case on Indian imports still pending. The boardroom is emptying: CEO and CFO both left 20 Mar 2026; the CPO in June — three senior exits in six months. Not cheap on persistent numbers: 17.7x on peak-cycle FY26 earnings; only ~₹2,780 on normalised mid-cycle earnings.

What would make the short fail: the CBP appeal succeeds; cell integration lifts margins back above 20%; the order book converts cleanly; FIIs keep accumulating.

Fair assessment: the cash-conversion and margin arguments hold up on the numbers — strongest. The US-trade argument is real but contested (CBP confirmed no Chinese cells in verified shipments and declined a blanket adverse-inference ruling). The governance argument is serious but not a formal red flag. A short would likely be right on the facts, wrong on the timing.
